Legal & commercial investigation

What Spotify's terms allow, what they forbid, and what each version of this POC costs as a result. Findings from reading the published terms and testing the endpoints directly — not legal advice.

Summary

Two requirements of this product conflict with Spotify's terms: hiding the track and charging for it. Neither is a technical limitation, and neither can be engineered around. The user cap that first blocked us is the least of the three.

1. The widget may not be obscured

The Embeds terms require that you

"display the Spotify Widgets in the form made available by Spotify, without alteration"

and that you not

"obfuscate the Spotify Widgets in any way, whether by banner advertisements or by any other means."

A blur under an opaque veil is obfuscation, and "by any other means" closes the gap. This is what v0.2 does, which is why it cannot ship.

Placing the widget further down a page is not obfuscation: it renders at full size, unaltered, and an ordinary scroll reaches it. That is the basis for v0.3. Pushing it off-screen instead — negative offsets, zero height — would put you back in breach.

2. Playback must show cover art and metadata

The Developer Policy states:

"metadata and cover art may only be used in connection with the underlying content, and there shall be no playback of Spotify Content without showing relevant cover art and metadata."

Attribution with the Spotify Marks and a link back to the track are required too. This is what rules out v0.1 as designed: owning the interface means taking on the duty to display what is playing.

3. The play button may not be used commercially

Section IV.3.e of the Embeds terms:

"the Spotify Play Button may not be used for commercial purposes. This restriction does not prohibit you from embedding the Spotify Play Button on Websites that contain advertising."

The carve-out shows where the line sits: the page around the button may earn money, but the button itself may not be the product. The test is whether money changes hands because of the playback.

  • Free app, ads on the page — explicitly permitted
  • Free app, no monetisation — fine
  • Paid or subscription app whose sessions play through the embed
  • Selling printed cards whose QR opens the embed — the cardboard is worthless without the playback it unlocks

The Developer Policy compounds this separately, barring the sale of streaming applications, in-app purchases within streaming features, and advertising sold on the streaming experience.

"Commercial purposes" is left undefined. That vagueness is not neutral: Spotify interprets it, and can withdraw access without a court being involved.

4. Endpoints restricted since 27 November 2024

Apps registered on or after that date — this one included — receive a 403 from Audio Features, Audio Analysis, Recommendations, Related Artists, Featured Playlists, Category Playlists, and the algorithmic and Spotify-owned editorial playlists.

Consequences: no tempo, key, energy or valence for a detail card; and no Spotify radio, because the algorithmic radio playlists are exactly what is withheld. A homemade radio from artist top-tracks and genre search remains possible. Apps that held extended access before the change kept it; new grants are not being made.

5. The user cap

In development mode only allowlisted accounts can sign in, each needing its own Premium subscription. Extended quota is applied for by form, reviewed against criteria Spotify says may change at any time, with no guaranteed timeline, and covers only the use case described. Describing this use case honestly means describing an app that hides metadata.

This cap applies to OAuth against the client ID. The embed, oEmbed and plain track deeplinks touch none of it and are unlimited.

6. Technical findings

  • open.spotify.com sends frame-ancestors 'self' — the real web player cannot be embedded in an iframe, at all
  • open.spotify.com/embed/… sends no framing restriction — the official widget frames freely
  • The oEmbed endpoint is fully public: title, cover art and iframe URL with no token, no quota and no account
  • The IFrame API is provided so the widget can be driven from your own controls — external play/pause buttons are sanctioned, not a workaround

8. Questions to put to Spotify

  • Does a paid subscription app whose content plays through the embed count as commercial use of the Play Button?
  • Does selling physical cards that link to an embed page count as commercial use?
  • Is an embed below the fold, unaltered and reachable by scrolling, acceptable where the intent is that the track is not seen first?
  • Is extended quota available at all to an app registered after November 2024 for this use case?

9. The alternative

Licensed, self-hosted audio removes every constraint above: no OAuth, no user cap, no metadata duty, no commercial restriction, no endpoint deprecations, and no party that can withdraw access. For ambient and guided material rather than chart music, it is also the more natural catalogue. The card codes, QR generation and page structure in this repo carry over unchanged.
